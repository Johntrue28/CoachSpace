# CoachSpace — Validation Plan (two weeks)

> A practical plan for a student founder: **Tuesday 13 October to Monday 26 October 2026**, with a go or no-go decision on the 26th. Sized for about 6–8 hours a week next to coursework; if time runs short, cut the number of interviews, never the quality of the questions. It serves the targets in `PRODUCT.md` §9 (5–10 interviews, 2–3 pilot commitments, at least one willingness-to-pay signal) and the Phase 2 exit decision in `ROADMAP.md`. Segment reasoning is in [`PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md`](PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md); the demo's known gaps are in [`PRODUCT_AUDIT.md`](PRODUCT_AUDIT.md).

That plan, with the emails, the tracking sheet, a 14-day schedule (14–27 October) and go and no-go criteria for email replies, is in [`POST_DEMO_AUDIT.md`](POST_DEMO_AUDIT.md) §8–§13. The interview guide (§6), the demo script (§7), the willingness-to-pay ladder (§8) and the signals table (§9) stay valid for any coach who offers a conversation. This also revises D-019 (interviews before the demo is shown), which is still recorded as Accepted until the owner decides.

## 1. What you are trying to learn

1. Do coaches with several recurring groups have a **frequent, costly problem** coordinating changes and knowing who is coming? (H1 and H2 in the strategy.)
2. Do they **already solve it well enough** with WhatsApp and a club system?
3. Will anyone **commit something real**: time, data, a pilot, money?

Prototype features are secondary. Everything else (chat, library, notes) is explored only after the problem is clear.

**Write the decision thresholds in §10 on paper before the first call**, so the evidence cannot be reinterpreted afterwards.

## 2. The first niche to test

**Independent tennis coaches** who run three or more recurring groups a week in venues they do not own (strategy §3). Reasons: best fit with the product as built, the simplest pilot, reachable people, and a plausible reason to pay. This is a hypothesis; the plan below includes a contrast interview so that a wrong niche shows up.

**Target mix for five interviews:**

| Interviews | Who | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Independent tennis coaches with three or more groups | The main test |
| 1 | Head coach or coordinator of a small tennis club or academy | The club-pays question and who decides |
| 1 | A contrast: a Pilates instructor or a youth team coach | Checks whether the niche choice is right |

## 3. Before the first call (about two hours)

- Open the live demo on your phone and check it loads; use **Reset demo data** before every demo.
- Practise the three-minute script (§7) aloud until it takes three minutes.
- Prepare a one-page notes sheet (§11) and a tracking sheet for prospects.
- Decide the language of each call; translate the message in §5 if needed.
- Optional, if time allows: the demo fixes DF-02, DF-03, DF-04 in `FEATURE_ROADMAP.md`. If not done, say aloud that seeded video links open a search page and that the data is fictional.
- Be ready to say plainly that **CoachSpace is a prototype and nothing in it is sent to anyone**. That is the project's own honesty rule (`CLAUDE.md` §8).

## 4. Finding 10 prospects

Aim for ten qualified names by Wednesday 14 October. Contact all ten plus five reserves. I cannot say what reply rate to expect; plan to treat three to five conversations as a good result and adjust on Monday 19 October.

| Where to look | How |
|---|---|
| Tennis clubs and halls you can reach | Club websites often name their coaches; contact the coach, not the front desk. Visit when courts are in use and ask a coach for twenty minutes after a session. |
| Coaches' own pages | Public Instagram, Facebook or website pages of local coaches who advertise group lessons. |
| Your network | Ask people who play tennis who coaches their group, and for an introduction. Remote calls are fine, in any country. |
| Players' community groups | Ask a member to introduce their coach. |
| University or town sports centre | Instructors in tennis, Pilates and group fitness. |
| The contrast | Two Pilates instructors or youth team coaches from the same sources. |

**Three-question screener** (ask before booking): How many recurring groups do you coach each week? Do you own or rent the venue? How do participants find out about changes today?

**Tracking sheet columns:** name, how found, segment, groups per week, venue, contacted on, replied, interview date, status.

## 5. Outreach message

Keep it short, honest and free of selling.

> Hi [name], I'm a student building a tool for coaches who run several groups, and I'm trying to understand how coaches handle schedules, changes and attendance today. I'm not selling anything. Could I ask you 20–30 minutes of questions about how you run your groups? I'm happy to share what I learn from other coaches.

> Hola [nombre], soy estudiante y estoy creando una herramienta para entrenadores con varios grupos. Quiero entender cómo gestionáis hoy los horarios, los cambios y la asistencia. No vendo nada. ¿Podría hacerte unas preguntas durante 20–30 minutos sobre cómo llevas tus grupos? Con gusto te cuento lo que aprenda de otros entrenadores.

## 6. Running an interview (30 minutes)

| Minutes | Part |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Thank them, say it is a prototype and you are learning, ask permission to take notes (do not record unless they agree). |
| 3–15 | Their last two weeks, in specific stories. |
| 15–20 | Tools and costs: ask them to show you the group chats and calendar they actually use. |
| 20–25 | The three-minute demo, then one task. |
| 25–30 | The commitment ask (§8). |

**Questions that avoid leading** (ask about the past, not the future):

1. Walk me through the last time a session was cancelled or moved. What happened from the moment you knew?
2. How did each person find out? Did anyone not? What happened?
3. Which tools did you use this week to run your groups? Can you show me?
4. When did you last find out only on arrival that someone was not coming?
5. Where do you keep attendance, and what do you do with it?
6. When did you last share a drill or video? How, and what happened next?
7. How much time a week goes on messages and schedule admin? Which part do you like least?
8. What have you tried to improve this? What did you stop using, and why?
9. What do you pay for any tool today?
10. Who decides which tools you use: you, a club, someone else?

After the demo: "Show me how you would have handled your last cancellation in this." Then: "What would have to be true for you to use it with one real group next week?"

**Do not ask:** "Would you use this?", "Is this a good idea?", "Would you pay €X?" as a hypothetical, or suggest solutions before they have described the problem. Do not argue with a criticism; ask for the example.

## 7. The three-minute demo

Use your phone in portrait, the Junior Tennis group, and the scenario "rain tomorrow". Let the coach hold the phone if they want.

| Time | Do | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Open the dashboard | "This shows next session and who is coming." Point at the next-session card. Say it is a prototype with fictional data. |
| 0:20 | Open a session; cancel it with a reason | "In the real version everyone affected would be told and could answer. In this prototype nothing is sent." |
| 1:10 | Open attendance; mark all present; tap two as absent; save | "This takes a few taps standing at the court." |
| 1:50 | Open the library; assign a drill to two groups with different instructions | "One drill, reused with different instructions per group." |
| 2:30 | Stop | Ask: "What is missing for you to use this with your Tuesday group?" Then be quiet. |

Do not narrate features; the point is their reaction. If they ask about chat or payments, note it and move on.

## 8. Testing willingness to pay

Use a ladder. Each step is harder to give than a compliment.

| Step | Ask | Counts as |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Time | "Can I come back in a week with a version that handles your Tuesday group's changes?" | A second call booked in the diary |
| 2. Data | "Could you send me your real weekly timetable, so I can set it up?" | A real file or screenshot |
| 3. Pilot | "Would you run one group on it from [date], for two weeks, and tell me what breaks?" | A named group and start date |
| 4. Introduction | "Who else coaches like you who should see this?" | A named person |
| 5. Money | "The paid version would be €19.90 a month; I'm offering pilot coaches €9.90 for the first six months. Would you pre-order for [date]?" | A pre-order, a signed letter of intent, or a clear refusal with a reason |

Ask the price directly and then stay silent. If they hesitate, ask what they pay today for any tool and what would make the price feel fair. Do not discount in the conversation. Do not take payment: pre-orders and letters of intent are enough, because nothing real can be delivered yet and there is no billing in the product (`PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` §8). All prices here are hypotheses.

## 9. Real interest or politeness

| Genuine signal | Polite signal |
|---|---|
| Tells a specific story from the last month without being prompted | "Interesting idea", "looks great" |
| Describes a workaround they built and how long it takes | "I'd definitely use something like this" with no date |
| Asks when it will be ready, or what it costs, before you mention it | Asks for the link and nothing else |
| Gives real data, an introduction, a date, or money | Promises to "think about it" |
| Raises problems you did not see | Only compliments |
| Pushes back on price with a reason | Agrees to everything |

## 10. Evidence and decisions

Write these down before the first call. They are small-sample qualitative thresholds, not statistics.

| Outcome | Condition | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| **Go** (propose a narrow pilot to the owner) | At least three of five describe a concrete schedule or attendance problem from the last 30 days, **and** at least two commit to a dated pilot with a real group, **and** at least one pre-orders or signs a letter of intent | Ask the owner for the Phase 3 decision (`ROADMAP.md`); size the pilot in `FEATURE_ROADMAP.md` §2 |
| **Revise** | Pain is recognised by three or more, but fewer than two pilot commitments | Change the pitch and demo, keep the niche, run five more interviews |
| **Change niche** | The contrast interview shows clearly stronger pain and commitment than tennis, or tennis coaches report their current tools work well | Repeat the plan for the stronger segment (`PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` §9) |
| **Stop or pause** | Fewer than two of five recount a concrete recent problem and nobody commits to anything | Pause and record the reason in `DECISIONS.md` |
| **Extend** | Fewer than three interviews happened by Monday 26 October | Extend a week; do not decide on missing evidence |

**Feature-level rules:**

- Defer chat if fewer than two of five want to move conversations off WhatsApp.
- Defer attendance if fewer than two keep any record today.
- Defer the library and plans if fewer than two share drills or videos regularly.
- Promote a feature one coach asks for only when a second asks without prompting.

## 12. Ethics and data

- Ask permission to take notes; record only with explicit agreement.
- Keep names and contact details in a private file; delete them after the decision unless the person agrees to be kept informed.
- Never put a real participant's data into the demo. Do not ask about individual minors; ask about the coach's process.
- Say that CoachSpace is a prototype and that nothing in it is sent.

## 13. Budget

Between nothing and about €50: coffee for a few conversations and, if you want, a custom domain (DF-01). No paid tools are needed.
